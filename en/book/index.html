The Regressive Backbone

Learn to feel statistics. Learn to read research.

A statistics book — from difference and the mean to correlation, multiple linear regression and logistic regression, explained in plain language.

Statistics has a reputation for being cold. Formulas, tests, tables — something for people with a knack for it. I don’t believe a word of it. Statistics is about difference, and you could feel difference before you could talk. That’s where this book begins: first the nerves out, then the material in.

NL·EN

The backbone in the title is regression: the movement back. A cloud of points comes home to a single line — the mean, only a slightly different one at every point along it. Each chapter adds a vertebra. The subtitle is the promise: learn to feel statistics, learn to read research. First feel what a mean, a relationship, a model is; after that, a results section turns out to be perfectly readable.

This book takes uncertainty and doubt seriously, precisely to stay in touch with reality.

The vertebrae

They come in order, and the order matters. Just start at chapter 0 — even if you already know some statistics. Below, each chapter has the statistics beside it, in plain terms, so you can see at a glance what it’s about: if you’re looking for covariance, you don’t need to know the chapter is called The Little Rectangle. The practice books

Learn to do statistics, and to write down what you found

This is where you get your hands on the controls. Every chapter above has a practice book that goes with it: the same material, the same order, the same numbers — but now you run it yourself, in the tool you use. You shuffle the data to see what chance alone can do, and for each chapter you can download the dataset.
