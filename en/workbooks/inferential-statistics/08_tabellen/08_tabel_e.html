Table E · the F-distribution

critical values, with two kinds of degrees of freedom

The table gives the critical value F*F^*: to its right lies probability p.

F is a fraction. Above the line is a variance with its own degrees of freedom (the numerator), below it a variance with different ones (the denominator). That is why table E has two kinds of df:

  • the numerator runs sideways along the top;
  • the denominator runs down the left, and for each denominator there are five probabilities p, one under the other.

In the weeks of this book you don’t calculate with F. You do come across it in the output of other programs: with Levene’s test from week 3 there is an F in it. Later, in analysis of variance, F takes the leading role.

Bracketing a p

Suppose you find F = 12.00 with 2 and 6 degrees of freedom: 2 for the numerator, 6 for the denominator. Go down the left to the block for denominator 6, and then across to column 2. There it says 10.92 at .010 and 27.00 at .001. 12.00 lies between them, so

.001<p<.010 .001 < p < .010

Don’t swap the two df: with 6 and 2 you would be looking in a completely different block.

Your denominator isn’t in the table? Take the next smaller one. For denominator 44 that is 40, just as with table D: with fewer degrees of freedom the boundary lies further out, and you are on the cautious side.

NoteCheck in R

qf() gives the critical value, pf() the probability in the tail. First the numerator, then the denominator.

qf(.01, df1 = 2, df2 = 6, lower.tail = FALSE)
[1] 10.92477
pf(12.00, df1 = 2, df2 = 6, lower.tail = FALSE)
[1] 0.008
